# Real-Time Identity Resolution & Bot Detection — Product Requirements

## Problem Statement

Clickstream data (device IDs, cookies, account logins, IP, timing) arrives as fragmented signals with no reliable real-time answer to "is this one real traveler across multiple devices, or many fake identities generated by one bot?" That ambiguity causes two concrete, dollar-measurable problems today:

1. **Marketing attribution fraud** — click-fraud and scraper bots pose as unique visitors. Their clicks/conversions get credited in attribution models alongside real travelers, inflating CAC and distorting campaign ROAS. Ad spend decisions get made on polluted data.
2. **Scraping / inventory-probing abuse** — price-scraping and competitor bots rotate cookies and devices to look like many distinct shoppers, evading per-user rate limits, adding unnecessary load to search/pricing backends, and polluting the behavioral data personalization/ML models train on.

Both problems need the same underlying primitive — linking device/cookie/account signals into a unified identity in real time, then scoring that identity cluster for bot-likelihood. Today each symptom is investigated separately and after the fact (attribution clawbacks, backend capacity firefighting), with no shared, real-time signal feeding either team.

## Goal

Maintain a real-time, unified view of "who is probably the same visitor" across device/cookie/account signals, and flag identity clusters that are probably automated — fast enough to act on *before* the thing it would prevent completes (before a click is credited to a campaign, before a scrape finishes, before a fraudulent account/promo redemption is created).

## Non-Goals

- **Not a final fraud/bot decision engine.** The pipeline produces a unified identity ID and a bot-likelihood score with signal breakdown; downstream systems (attribution pipeline, trust & safety, rate limiter) decide what to do with it — exclude, flag for review, throttle. This pipeline does not block, discount, or auto-ban anything itself.
- **Not authenticated cross-device identity verification.** Scope is anonymous/semi-anonymous signal stitching (device, cookie, IP) with account ID as one more linking signal — not a replacement for loyalty-account identity/CRM matching.
- **Not real-time enforcement (WAF/rate-limiting).** This is a signal-producing pipeline; enforcement is a separate, future consumer.

## Target Users

| User | Need |
|---|---|
| Marketing analytics / ad ops | Clean attribution data — bot-driven clicks/conversions excluded or flagged before they're credited to a campaign |
| Trust & safety / fraud team | Early signal on bot clusters probing accounts or promos, before a fraud ring fully forms |
| Search/pricing platform team | Visibility into which load is scraper-driven, so rate-limiting/caching can target actual bot clusters instead of raw IPs (which legitimate users share too) |
| Personalization / ML team | Cleaner training data — bot sessions excluded from "what do real travelers do" signals |

## Scope — v1

- Real-time identity resolution via incremental union-find over device_id / cookie_id / account_id co-occurrence, in Flink keyed state.
- Bot-likelihood scoring per identity cluster from three signals: identity fan-out (too many device/cookie IDs merging into one cluster too fast), shared-IP concentration, and click-timing regularity (inter-event intervals too fast or too mechanically even for a human).
- Event-time processing with watermarks, tolerating out-of-order delivery across the three signal sources.
- Fault-tolerant, exactly-once processing (RocksDB-backed keyed state, Flink checkpointing), with a TTL-bounded identity graph so state doesn't grow unbounded.
- Output: unified identity ID + bot score (with signal breakdown), published to Kafka for downstream consumption.

## Out of Scope — v1 (future consideration)

- Automated enforcement actions (auto-excluding attribution, auto-throttling) based on bot score — v1 only emits the signal.
- Cross-brand identity stitching (e.g. linking identity across separate sibling brands/domains) — single brand/domain scope only.
- Human-in-the-loop review UI for disputed bot flags.

## Success Metrics

| Category | Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Identity resolution quality | % of known same-person multi-device sessions correctly merged into one cluster (validated against logged-in account_id as ground truth where available) | Confirms the union-find linking actually finds real people, not just device noise |
| Identity resolution quality | False-merge rate — unrelated people incorrectly unified (e.g. shared household/office IP) | Over-merging is the dangerous failure mode: it mislabels real distinct shoppers as one bot-like fan-out cluster |
| Bot detection quality | Bot-flagged traffic as % of total sessions, trended over time | Primary signal that the pipeline is finding meaningful bot volume, not noise |
| Bot detection quality | False-positive rate on known-good high-volume users (travel agents, API/affiliate partners with naturally bursty or regular traffic) | Fan-out and timing-regularity signals can misfire on legitimate bulk users — this must stay bounded |
| Business impact | Ad spend attributed to flagged-bot clicks/conversions (estimated $ excluded from attribution) | Direct dollar tie-back to the attribution-fraud problem |
| Business impact | Scraper-attributed request volume on search/pricing APIs (%) | Direct tie-back to the scraping-abuse problem; informs rate-limit/caching capacity planning |
| Operational | End-to-end latency: event ingested → unified identity + bot score available downstream | Must land before the thing it's meant to prevent completes (ad credit, scrape completion) |
| Operational / resilience | Zero duplicate or lost output records across a TaskManager failure (chaos-tested) | Correctness under failure is a hard requirement — this feeds attribution-adjacent decisions, not just a dashboard |
| Operational | Identity graph state size per key, bounded under TTL | Confirms TTL is actually bounding state growth rather than accumulating indefinitely |

## Open Questions

- What's the acceptable false-positive ceiling before legitimate shared-IP households, corporate NAT, or VPN users get miscategorized as a bot cluster — and who owns tuning that threshold?
- Does unifying cookie + device + account identity into a persisted graph (even TTL-bounded) need its own privacy/consent/retention review, separate from the consent already covering the underlying event collection?
- When an identity cluster is found to be a false merge after the fact, is there a correction/replay path for downstream consumers, or does it just decay out via TTL?
